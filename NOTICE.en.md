# Sources, rights and credits

This project reconstructs the argument of a book under copyright. It does not reproduce its text: it **paraphrases** it, pointing to each passage, and **quotes** only short extracts. **To read Lordon, read Lordon.**

## The book

- Frédéric Lordon, *Capitalisme, désir et servitude. Marx et Spinoza*, Paris, La Fabrique, 2010. © Frédéric Lordon and La Fabrique éditions.
- English translation: *Willing Slaves of Capital. Spinoza and Marx on Desire*, trans. Gabriel Ash, London, Verso, 2014.
- What the repository contains of the book: short quotations (433 passages, almost all a few words long; at most 29 consecutive words of Lordon), each with its locator (section and paragraph), and the titles of its sections, used as locators. The text itself is not distributed: the `source/` folder is excluded from the repository, and `outils/check.py` checks that it stays so.
- These quotations fall under the right of short quotation (French Intellectual Property Code, art. L122-5, 3°): they are incorporated into a work of analysis and their source is given. They are **not** covered by the repository's licences (see `LICENSE`).

## The English version

- The English version translates the reconstruction (titles, statements, demonstrations, comments). It was made from the French reconstruction, not from the published English translation, which is neither used nor quoted.
- In the English bodies, Lordon's words, where quoted, are our own short renderings of the French, with the same locators; the `citation` field keeps the original French.
- Section titles and locators (§ 08.4) refer to the French edition.

## Spinoza

- Links to the *Ethics* point to [SpinozaGraph](https://github.com/mqtin/SpinozaGraph) (online: <https://treillis.qpetit.com/spinoza/>), which publishes the Latin text and public-domain translations, including Elwes's English. Nothing from SpinozaGraph is copied here.
- The statements of the S premises are paraphrases of Spinoza. The quotations from Spinoza are those Lordon makes, in the translations he uses: Robert Misrahi for the *Ethics* (protected translation), Charles Ramond for the *Political Treatise* (protected translation), Charles Appuhn for a few letters and passages (public domain). They are short and sourced.

## Other authors

Quotations from Marx and Engels, Kant, Pascal, Camus, Bourdieu, Deleuze, Balibar and others are those Lordon reports, with the reference he gives in his notes.
